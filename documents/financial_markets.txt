Introduction to Financial Markets

Financial markets are venues where buyers and sellers trade assets such as stocks, bonds, currencies, and commodities. Stock markets allow companies to raise capital by selling ownership shares to investors, while bond markets let governments and companies borrow money by issuing debt that pays periodic interest.

Prices in financial markets are influenced by a mix of factors, including company earnings, interest rates set by central banks, inflation expectations, and broader economic data. Investor sentiment can also cause prices to move sharply in the short term, even when underlying fundamentals change more slowly.

Long term investors often focus on diversification, spreading money across different asset classes to reduce risk, rather than trying to predict short term price movements. Understanding basic concepts like risk, return, and compounding is essential before making investment decisions in any financial market.
